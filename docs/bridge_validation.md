## Cost at 120 papers

The measured single pass costs **$27.45 projected**, or **$43.75 reserved at dispatch**. It comprises one
outline, 10 sections and the front matter, one integration pass, one critique and the LINE and PROOF edits.

| Line (request-bound, approved plan) | Calls | USD |
|---|---:|---:|
| A08 | 1 | 1.79 |
| A09 (every section with 2 retrieval rounds, every section redrafted once) | 62 | 124.52 |
| A11 (2 cycles, on Kimi: counted as over the window) | 2 | 2.84 |
| A12 (integration per draft, 2 revisions, LINE, PROOF) | 6 | 8.52 |

The A09 line is large because every retrieval round and every redraft resends the section's packet and all
earlier prose, at 0.5–0.8 MB each. This cost follows the review's length, not the paper count: two papers
still project $42.44 for A09. `bridge.max_retrieval_rounds` and `loops.max_redrafts` set it, and changing
either is a scope decision for the researcher. The A07 and A10 envelopes are not this task's and are
unchanged ($5,492.64 and $158.34 at 1,996 findings, per the integrated audit).

## The comparative table

At 120 papers, 54 findings are contrary evidence: used as CONFLICTING, or named by a reconciliation issue.
One page holds 25 rows, so 29 contrary findings cannot appear in the table. The builder therefore applies this rule:

- contrary findings come first;
- no supporting row appears without every contrary finding of the propositions it supports;
- contrary findings that do not fit are listed in `contrary_findings_not_shown`, and the prose carries them,
  since they are mandatory elements.

This reads "contrary evidence is always included" as "no claim is shown without its contrary evidence". If
it must mean every contrary finding in the table, a 120-paper review needs more than one page, which is a
journal-format decision.
